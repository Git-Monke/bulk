Looking at this, the unpolished feeling comes from a few specific things working against each other:
Visual hierarchy is flat. Almost everything is the same shade of gray on slightly-different gray. The recipe cards, meal cards, and summary cards all use nearly identical backgrounds and borders. Your eye has nowhere to land first. Good interfaces guide attention; this one just presents a wall of equally-weighted information.
Typography is doing one thing. Same font, same weight pattern repeated everywhere. The numbers (which are the actual content people care about — calories, macros, cost) aren't visually distinguished from the labels. Compare to how Linear or Notion treat numerical data: tabular figures, often a different weight or slight color shift to make them scannable.
Spacing is uniform but not intentional. Everything has roughly the same padding. There's no rhythm — no sense that related things are tightly grouped and unrelated things are separated. The meal cards inside Day A blend into each other because the gap between meals is barely larger than the gap between items within a meal.
The progress bars look like 2008. Those gray slider tracks with the little square handles are the single most dated element. Modern equivalents are thinner, often colored, sometimes just a subtle fill behind text.
Color is almost entirely absent, then jarring when present. The only colors are the red "Clear" and red X buttons. Red is the most aggressive color you can pick, and you've used it for both a destructive bulk action and routine item removal. Meanwhile there's no accent color doing positive work anywhere — no brand color, no highlight on the active state, nothing.
Borders everywhere. Every card has a 1px border. Stack enough bordered boxes and it starts looking like a spreadsheet. Modern UIs lean on background color shifts and shadows instead, or use borders much more sparingly.
The "Bulk" logo/header is tiny and the salad emoji-thing reads as placeholder art. It signals "I haven't gotten to branding yet."
The functionality looks solid — the information architecture is actually pretty good. It's purely a visual design problem. If you tightened the type system, introduced one accent color, replaced the sliders, and varied the card backgrounds (e.g., meal cards slightly inset/darker than the page, summary cards with a subtle elevation), it would feel dramatically more finished without changing any logic.
